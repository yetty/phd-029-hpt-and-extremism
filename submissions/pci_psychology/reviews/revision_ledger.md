This ledger converts every actionable point in the recommender letter and the
two reviews into a revision, rationale, and acceptance check. It does not
record a comment as resolved merely because related text exists. A point is
resolved only when the proposed revision is present and the validation check
passes in the rebuilt manuscript.

## Status key

- **Unresolved:** the requested change is absent.
- **Partly addressed:** relevant material exists, but it does not fully answer
  the comment.
- **Appears addressed:** the submitted text already contains the requested
  feature; it still needs explicit confirmation in the response letter.
- **Evidence unavailable:** the requested information was not collected or
  cannot be established from current project files. The revision must state
  that transparently rather than inventing evidence.

## Priority 1: Validity claims and factual accuracy

### REC-01 - Treat the single scenario as a content-validity limitation

**Source:** Recommender, paragraph 2

**Required revision:** Explain why one Weimar scenario cannot establish broad
content-based validity evidence for the domain of historical perspective
taking. Separate construct underrepresentation through limited task sampling
from the existing participant-sampling limitation.

**Baseline status:** **Unresolved.** Section 5.6, limitation 1, combines a
single country and a single scenario in two sentences. It does not discuss
content representation, domain sampling, or the boundary between evidence for
this task and evidence for HPT more broadly.

**User decision (2026-09-10):** **Accept stronger revision.** Add a dedicated
content-based validity limitation and bound claims consistently across the
abstract, discussion, recommendations, and conclusion. Include a future
multi-scenario content blueprint. No new analysis or data collection is
required.

**Recommended refinement:** Give content validity its own limitation. State
that repeated ratings of one vignette increase item count without broadening
the sampled content domain. Bound score interpretations to the Weimar task or
closely related scenarios. Explain that stronger evidence would require an
explicit content blueprint derived from the construct definition and multiple
scenarios that vary relevant features, potentially including periods, places,
actors, moral valences, and levels of contextual familiarity. Carry the same
boundary into Sections 5.5, 5.7, and 5.8. A fixed vignette may help hold content
constant, but this is a design rationale rather than demonstrated evidence of
construct isolation.

**Validation check:**

- [ ] Content validity has a dedicated paragraph or numbered limitation.
- [ ] The text distinguishes multiple items from multiple content samples.
- [ ] Claims are bounded to the present scenario or task family.
- [ ] Future work specifies a multi-scenario content blueprint.
- [ ] The abstract, discussion, and conclusion contain no broader claim that
  contradicts this limitation.

### REC-02 - Strengthen and bound discriminant-validity claims

**Source:** Recommender, paragraph 3

**Required revision:** Do not treat superior three-factor fit and factor

**Baseline status:** **Unresolved.** Section 4.1 explicitly says that

discriminant" without requiring external criteria.

**User decision (2026-09-10):** **Downgrade the claim and run exploratory

internal-structure language. Separately test whether POP, ROA, and CONT show
different relations with the available historical-knowledge and history-grade

**Validation check:**

### REC-05 - Reframe brief-scale consistency and its validity consequences

**Source:** Recommender, paragraph 6; reinforced by Reviewer 1's discussion
comment

**Required revision:** Frame modest internal consistency through Loevinger's
attenuation paradox rather than simply as failure against conventional
benchmarks, while acknowledging that low precision constrains validity
estimates.

**Baseline status:** **Partly addressed.** Sections 4.3 and 5.3 explain the
three-item constraint, and Section 5.6 limits individual score interpretation.
However, Section 5.3 still opens with failure against conventional benchmarks,
does not cite Loevinger, and claims the observed correlations demonstrate
"genuine psychometric signal." The reliability of the ideology composite is
also unreported, weakening the disattenuated estimate.

**User decision (2026-09-10):** **Verify, then cite Loevinger.** Retrieve and
read the relevant source or a reliable modern treatment before adding the
citation. Reframe the discussion around the construct-breadth versus
score-precision trade-off, distinguish that paradox from ordinary statistical
attenuation, and compute reliability for FR-LF, KSA-3, and the combined
ideology score. Retain the disattenuated estimate only if it is reproducible
and defensible after those checks.

**Recommended refinement:** Verify and read an appropriate source before
citing the attenuation paradox; the local Loevinger note is currently only a
bibliographic stub. Explain the trade-off: narrow, internally homogeneous
items can raise consistency while narrowing construct representation, whereas
brief heterogeneous subscales may preserve breadth but attenuate associations.
Then state directly that measurement error limits correlations, group
comparisons, and individual interpretation. Report reliability for all focal
predictor scales and the composite used for disattenuation, or remove the
disattenuated claim if it cannot be supported.

**Validation check:**

- [ ] Any Loevinger claim is verified against the original or a reliable
  modern treatment.
- [ ] Reliability is framed as a breadth-precision trade-off, not excused.
- [ ] Consequences for validity coefficients are explicit.
- [ ] Reliability is reported for FR-LF, KSA-3, and the ideology composite.
- [ ] The disattenuated estimate is reproducible or removed.

## Recommended revision sequence

1. Verify the scenario wording against the administered instrument and source.
2. Rewrite content-validity, discriminant-validity, reliability, and claim
   boundaries before making cosmetic changes.
3. Run the additional reporting analyses: scale reliability, correlation
   intervals, participant distributions, and baseline plots.
4. Repair theory and context: mechanism, moral/affective boundary, Czech
   curriculum, and verified HPT follow-up literature.
5. Simplify tables, labels, Results signposting, research questions, and
   abstract.
6. Run a technical reproducibility audit, rebuild the PDF, and check every box
   in this ledger against the rendered document.
7. Draft a point-by-point response that cites ledger IDs and exact manuscript
   locations. Preserve disagreements as reasoned, evidence-based responses
   rather than silently declining a request.
