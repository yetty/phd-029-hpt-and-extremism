Overall, this is a quite thorough and well done development and validation of the historical
perspective taking (HPT) index translated and applied to the Czech context. To be honest,
as a researcher of U.S. political behavior that uses quite di erent methodologies and
statistical approaches to analysis, there is not much I can weigh in on regarding the
analysis provided. I encourage the author to seek out critique from reviewers with greater
knowledge in the methodology employed. The results section, again, is quite thorough, and
the methods sections adequate describe how the data and analytical approach can a)
validate the Czech HPT index, and b) assess if any variance in response across ideology
(even though there was not) is a result of construct-irrelevant variance - ideological
sympathy with the scenario presented, rather than nuanced and non-'presentist' historical
reasoning. The study also did well in emphasizing that the author's approach can extend
beyond HPT and the Czech context; a similar methodology can be used by researchers and
practioners seeking to validate any measure that contains political or moral content.
I have two quite small suggestions to o er (again, I would find other reviewers that could
dig into the methodology and technical aspects of the results more). First, in describing the
participants on pg. 13, I would provide more descriptive data on the students and schools.
For instance, what is the ideological distribution of the students? It is only mentioned in the
Discussion section (which 17% of students having above average right-wing sympathies),
but should be included earlier. It's the key independent variable being examined, so it
needs more discussion of its distribution up front. In addition, there is a mention of the
most recent history grade, but more detail on the distribution of students' historical
knowledge would be good to have. In addition, what is the socioeconomic background of
the schools sampled? Given that this is not a representative sample, that would be a good
statistic to include, in addition to the region and funding source of the schools.
Second, and this could be due to my lack of technical knowledge, but adding some simpler
presentations of the relationship between ideology and HPT, and historical knowledge and
HPT, would help (in addition to, not in replacement of, the great work done in the Methods
section). There are CFA scores, DIF scores, betas from regression models, etc. But just add
something more descriptive as well - a mean score for each ideology group, a data plot
with a fit line illustrating the relationshiop between HPT and ideology and/or knowledge,
etc. Adding a more 'naïve' or baseline representation of these key relationships could help
orient the reader as they dig into the more technical statistical analysis.
Again, this looks like fantastic work, and I enjoyed reading it!
